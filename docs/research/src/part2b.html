4The MVP, feature by feature

The MVP is the smallest release that keeps the product's promise: the apps a user chooses stay closed when they said they should, with a fair and limited way to open them. It has ten features. Each is specified the same way: what the user gets, how each platform does it, the rules, and the edge cases it must handle. Lines marked "Example" show one real set of choices, from an exercise app.

Design rule behind every feature: a block never starts without an end time the system already knows (an iOS schedule; on Android the plan plus the service clock). A crash, a dead battery or an abandoned workout can't leave someone locked out.

4.2 Feature specifications

M1 · Setup and permissions

User getsWhy → access → pick apps → always allowed → daily limit → lock times → strictness → summary. Nothing is blocked until the last step.
iOSOne Apple prompt, confirmed with Face ID or the passcode (individual authorization) [2].
AndroidA disclosure screen with consent [56], then Usage access, Display over other apps, notifications, and a battery step that opens the system battery page ("choose Don't optimise / Unrestricted") [43].
Edge casesA denied permission keeps setup on that step and leaves the rest of the app fully usable. Where blocking can't work (Expo Go, an iPhone build without the entitlement, iOS below 17.4) the feature is hidden.

M2 · App lists (blocked and always allowed)

User getsOne list of apps to block and one list that always stays open (Phone, Messages and Maps are suggested).
iOSApple's picker: apps, whole categories and websites, returned as anonymous tokens [4]. Categories are recommended.
AndroidThe app's own list of launchable apps with real names, using <queries> and not QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES [57].
RulesAlways-allowed wins over blocked. In Strict, apps can be added to the blocked list but not removed.
Edge casesiOS: more than 50 single apps silently blocks nothing [10][27], so every picker refuses 51 and suggests categories. iOS 26.5 can expire tokens [15]: a "Pick your apps again" banner appears. A new phone is a new setup (tokens are device-specific).

M3 · Focus session

User gets"Block my apps now, until this time." While it runs, a blocked app shows the session message and a button back to the app.
iOSA dedicated shield store, plus a system wake-up at the planned end so iOS lifts it even if the app is closed. Schedules must last 15 minutes [16], so the wake-up is a 15-minute window that ends at the planned end. The button opens the app on iOS 26.5+ [14] and just closes the blocked app on older versions.
AndroidThe plan carries "focus until T"; the service blocks until T, and the button opens the app.
RulesA running session can only be extended, never shortened. Earned minutes don't open apps during it; the emergency unlock still does. Nothing lasts longer than 3 hours. In Strict it can't be ended by hand.
ExampleAn exercise app's "Workout Focus": a chooser above Start with Off · This set · 15 · 30 · 45 · 60 min. "This set" ends when the set is done (safety cap 60 min); a timed focus runs its full time. The block screen says "Workout in progress" with Back to workout.
Edge casesApp crash or an abandoned session: the system lifts the block at the planned end. A clock moved backwards doesn't extend it. If the session can't start (for example a missing permission), nothing is blocked.
Open questionOn iPhone, whether the under-15-minute window lifts on time is Unverified on a device.

M4 · Lock times

User getsUp to 7 repeating windows by weekday, each at least 15 minutes, with ready-made presets. A lock time can block the main list or its own list, and can be marked "earned time can open it" or not.
iOSOne repeating DeviceActivity schedule per edge; the monitor extension applies and lifts the shield with the app closed [16][18]. Budget: 5 fixed activities + 2 per lock time = 19 of the 20 allowed.
AndroidThe service checks the schedule every second and restarts after a reboot [41].
RulesMaking a lock easier waits: until midnight in Standard, 24 hours in Strict. A running lock can't be shortened in Strict.
Edge casesWindows across midnight, daylight-saving changes and time-zone travel are covered by tests in three time zones. Schedules are re-registered on every app open, which also repairs schedules lost after an iPhone restart [29].

M5 · Daily limit

User getsA daily allowance for the blocked apps (default 60 min). At zero they're blocked until midnight, unless minutes were earned.
iOSUsage thresholds in 15-minute steps plus a "limit" event [17]. Because these events misfire [25][91], the extension rejects any event that arrives sooner than real time allows and re-arms the remainder (up to 3 times). Shown as approximate.
AndroidCounted to the second by the service.
Edge casesA false "limit reached" would lock someone out wrongly, so the guard prefers blocking a little late over blocking early. Moving the clock back never grants a fresh day.
Open questionHow the guard behaves over a real day on an iPhone is Unverified; it is the first item of the device pass.

M6 · Earn time with a task (optional)

User getsExtra minutes for doing something the app can verify, up to a daily cap. This is what separates a product from a plain blocker, and it's optional in a first release.
Both platformsPure app logic on top of M4 and M5: earned minutes keep the apps open after the daily limit and during lock times marked "earned time can open it".
RulesOnly verified work earns, each piece once. A daily cap. Earned minutes never open apps during a focus session.
ExampleCamera-verified sets: push-up 1 min per clean rep (0.5 if flagged), crunch 0.5 min (0.25), plank and side plank 2 min per 10 s in good form; 120 earned minutes a day. Demo sets and reps removed by the real-person check earn nothing.
Edge casesOn iOS, spending earned time is measured with the same misfire guard as M5. The block screen offers "Earn time" only when earning is actually possible (cap not reached, lock allows it).

M7 · Block screen

User getsWhy the app is closed ("Today's time is up", a lock time's name, "Workout in progress"), until when, and one or two buttons: Earn time, Back to the session, Emergency: 10 min, 1 more minute or Close.
iOSApple's shield with the app's text and two buttons [12]. On 26.5+ a button can open the app [14]; before that, "Earn time" closes the blocked app and the earn screen appears the next time the app is opened.
AndroidThe app's own full-screen page, opened from the background through the overlay permission [37]. Close goes to the home screen; the back gesture is handled.
Edge casesAndroid: the blocked app can be visible for up to about a second before the screen covers it. iOS: system apps can't be shielded [96].

M8 · Strictness and ways out

ModeChanging rulesWay out of a block
NudgeApplies at once"1 more minute", 3 times a day
Standard (default)Loosening waits until midnightOne 10-minute emergency unlock a day
Strict (1–30 days, default 7)Apps can't be removed, a running lock can't be shortened, loosening waits 24 hNo unlocks. Ending Strict early costs real effort (for example 100 verified push-ups)

The numbers are example defaults. All of this is rule logic in one shared engine, identical on both platforms. Every unlock has an end that is registered with the system, so the block comes back even if the app is closed.

M9 · Protection health (bypass detection)

User getsA red banner when blocking isn't working, and history lines such as "off 2 min" for the time it wasn't.
iOSThe authorization status is checked on every app open [85]; time without access is logged as a gap.
AndroidThe service writes a heartbeat every 5 seconds. A gap during which the screen was on is logged as "stopped" (the user stopped the app [40]) or "killed" (the system did). Lost permissions show the banner.
Edge casesA phone that was simply off or asleep is not counted as a bypass. Deleting the app can't be detected on either platform.

M10 · Home card, history and Turn off

User getsHome card: time used against the limit, earned minutes left, the next lock time, a running focus. Screen Time page: all settings and 14 days of history (used, earned, focus held, time off).
Turn offOne button removes every shield, schedule and the Android service, so nobody has to delete the app to escape. It follows the strictness rules (not available during Strict).
DataRules, history, tokens and app names stay on the phone (12.3).

4.5 Device pass: what must be seen on real phones

iPhone

  • Focus and lock time hold with the app force-quit, and lift on time.
  • A focus shorter than 15 minutes lifts at its planned end.
  • Daily limit over a full day: count early or duplicate events.
  • Revoke access: banner and history gap on next open.
  • "Turn off", then delete the app: nothing stays blocked.
  • Pick 49, 50 and 51 apps; token expiry on 26.5.
  • Restart the phone mid-lock.

Android

  • Force stop, Task Manager stop and Safe Mode: each shows as "off" afterwards.
  • Restart mid-lock: blocking resumes without opening the app.
  • Samsung and Xiaomi overnight with the battery saver on.
  • Split screen and picture-in-picture over a blocked app.
  • Revoke each permission in turn.
  • Advanced Protection on (Android 17): no effect expected, since no accessibility service is used.
