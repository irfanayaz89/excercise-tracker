4The MVP, feature by feature

The MVP is the smallest release that keeps the product's promise: the apps a user chooses stay closed when they said they should, with a fair and limited way to open them. Its ten features (M1–M10) are specified the same way: what the user gets, each platform, the rules and the edge cases. "Level" is the strictness level from section 7; lines marked "Example" show one real set of choices, from an exercise app.

Design rule behind every feature: a block never starts without an end time the system already knows (an iOS schedule; on Android the plan plus the service clock). A crash, a dead battery or an abandoned workout can't leave someone locked out.

4.2 Feature specifications

M1 · Setup and permissions

User getsWhy → access → pick apps → always allowed → daily limit → lock times → strictness → summary. Nothing is blocked until the last step.
iOSOne Apple prompt for individual authorization (5.2).
AndroidA disclosure screen with consent [56], then Usage access, Display over other apps, notifications, and the system battery page ("Don't optimise / Unrestricted") [43].
Edge casesA denied permission keeps setup on that step; the rest of the app stays usable. Where blocking can't work (Expo Go, an iPhone build without the entitlement, iOS below 17.4) the feature is hidden.

M2 · App lists

User getsA list of apps to block and a list that always stays open (Phone, Messages and Maps are suggested).
PlatformsiOS: Apple's picker returns apps, categories and websites as tokens [4]; categories are recommended. Android: the app's own list of launchable apps, using <queries>, not QUERY_ALL_PACKAGES [57].
RulesAlways-allowed wins over blocked. In Strict, apps can be added to the blocked list but not removed.
Edge casesiOS: more than 50 single apps silently blocks nothing, and one report says the 50 counts across all stores, a risk for separate block, pause and limit stores [10][27]; the picker refuses 51 and suggests categories. Expired tokens (26.5) show a "Pick your apps again" banner [15]. A new phone is a new setup (tokens are reportedly device-specific).

M3 · Focus session

User gets"Block my apps now, until this time." A blocked app shows the session message and a button back to the app.
iOSA dedicated shield store plus a system wake-up at the planned end. Schedules last at least 15 minutes [16], so the wake-up window starts in the past and ends at the planned end; intervalDidEnd runs then if the phone is in use, otherwise at its next use [120]. The button opens the app on 26.5+ [14] and closes the blocked app before that. Android: the plan carries "focus until T" and the button opens the app.
RulesA running session can be extended, never shortened. Earned minutes don't open apps during it; the emergency unlock does. At most 3 hours. In Strict it can't be ended by hand.
Example"Workout Focus": Off · This set · 15 · 30 · 45 · 60 min. "This set" ends with the set (safety cap 60 min). The block screen says "Workout in progress" with Back to workout.
Edge casesAfter a crash or an abandoned session the system lifts the block at the planned end. A clock moved backwards doesn't extend it. If the session can't start, nothing is blocked.

M4 · Lock times

User getsUp to 7 repeating weekday windows of at least 15 minutes, with presets. Each blocks the main list or its own, and is marked "earned time can open it" or not.
iOSOne repeating DeviceActivity schedule per edge [16][18]. Budget: 6 fixed activities (including the pause's wake-up, M12) + 2 per lock time = 20 of the 20 allowed. Android: the service checks every second and restarts after a reboot [39][44].
RulesLoosening waits until midnight in Standard and 24 hours in Strict. A running lock can't be shortened in Strict.
Edge casesWindows across midnight, daylight-saving changes and time-zone travel need tests in several time zones. Schedules are re-registered on every app open (5.7).

M5 · Daily limit

User getsA daily allowance for the blocked apps (default 60 min). At zero they're blocked until midnight, unless minutes were earned.
iOSUsage thresholds in 15-minute steps plus a "limit" event [17]. Because they misfire (5.5), the extension rejects any event that arrives sooner than real time allows and re-arms the remainder (up to 3 times). Shown as approximate. Android counts to the second.
Edge casesThe guard prefers blocking a little late over blocking early. Moving the clock back never grants a fresh day. How the guard behaves over a real day on an iPhone is Unverified.

M6 · Earn time with a task (optional)

User getsExtra minutes for work the app can verify, up to a daily cap. This separates a product from a plain blocker.
Both platformsApp logic on top of M4 and M5: earned minutes keep apps open after the daily limit and in lock times marked "earned time can open it". On iOS, spending is measured with M5's guard.
RulesOnly verified work earns, each piece once; a daily cap; never during a focus session. The block screen offers "Earn time" only when earning is possible.
ExampleCamera-verified sets: push-up 1 min per clean rep (0.5 if flagged), crunch 0.5 min (0.25), plank 2 min per 10 s; 120 min a day. A real-person check removes demo sets, which earn nothing.

M7 · Block screen

User getsWhy the app is closed ("Today's time is up", a lock time's name, "Workout in progress"), until when, and one or two of: Earn time, Back to the session, Emergency: 10 min, 1 more minute, Close.
PlatformsiOS: Apple's shield with the app's text and two buttons (5.4); before 26.5, "Earn time" closes the blocked app and the earn screen appears on the next open. Android: the app's own full-screen page, opened from the background through the overlay permission [37]; Close goes home and the back gesture is handled.
Edge casesAndroid: the blocked app can show for up to about a second. iOS: Always Allowed apps escape category and "everything" shields (5.4).

M8 · Strictness and ways out

ModeChanging rulesWay out of a block
NudgeApplies at once"1 more minute", 3 times a day
Standard (default)Loosening waits until midnightOne 10-minute emergency unlock a day
Strict (1–30 days, default 7)Apps can't be removed, a running lock can't be shortened, loosening waits 24 hNo unlocks. Ending Strict early costs real effort (for example 100 verified push-ups)

Example defaults. All of this is rule logic in one shared engine. Every unlock has an end registered with the system, so the block comes back even if the app is closed.

M9 · Protection health

User getsA red banner when blocking isn't working, and history lines such as "off 2 min".
PlatformsiOS: the launch-time authorization check (5.2); time without access is logged as a gap. Android: a heartbeat every 5 seconds; a gap with the screen on is logged as "stopped" or "killed" (6.3), and lost permissions show the banner.
Edge casesA phone that was off or asleep isn't a bypass. Deleting the app can't be detected on either platform.

M10 · Home card, history and Turn off

User getsHome card: time used against the limit, earned minutes left, the next lock time, a running focus. Screen Time page: all settings and 14 days of history (used, earned, focus held, time off).
Turn offOne button removes every shield, schedule and the Android service, so nobody has to delete the app to escape. It follows the strictness rules (not during Strict).
DataRules, history, tokens and app names stay on the phone (11.3).

4.3 Left out, and why

FeatureWhy it waits
Website blocking on AndroidNeeds a VPN, with unclear Play eligibility [58]. On iPhone, Apple's picker already has a Websites tab (5.4).
"Earn 5 / 15 / 60 min" menu on the iOS shieldiOS 26.4+ only [13]; two buttons already cover the flow.
Android screen pinningThe user confirms it and can leave with a gesture [46]; M13 does the job better.
Accessibility, VPN or device adminPlay policy risk, and Android 17 Advanced Protection switches off non-tool accessibility services [54][103].
Family or child mode; accountability partnerTurns the product into parental control, brings children's-privacy law (11.3), and on iOS usage data may go only to a parent through Family Sharing, never to another adult [23].
