1Scope and how to read this guide

This guide is for any team adding screen time and app blocking to an iOS or Android app. It defines a minimum viable product (MVP) feature by feature, and gives the platform detail, strictness limits and store rules behind it.

Sources: Apple and Google documentation, store policies and change logs, plus Apple Developer Forums and developer write-ups for behaviour the documentation doesn't cover; all read on 18–20 November 2026. Numbers like [12] link to the reference list.

Labels: Confirmed official Apple or Google documentation or policy · Apple staff an Apple engineer on the forums · Community developer reports; verify on devices · Unverified no reliable source.

2How app blocking works on phones

There are two fundamentally different models. Understanding them explains almost every difference between iOS and Android in this guide.

iOS: the system blocks, the app only gives instructions

  • The app tells iOS which apps to block and when.
  • iOS itself draws the block screen (the "shield") and keeps it up, even if the app is closed or force-quit.
  • The app never learns which apps the user picked, only anonymous tokens.

Android: the app blocks, using general permissions

  • There is no blocking API.
  • The app watches which app is on screen and covers it with its own screen.
  • It sees real app names and exact usage. The block only works while the app's background service is running.
