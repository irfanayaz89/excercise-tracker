11Security, privacy and store compliance

11.1 Apple: entitlement, review and the developer agreement

  1. Paid team. Family Controls (development) is only for paid Apple Developer Program members [8]; a free Personal team can't use it.
  2. Distribution request. The Account Holder submits the Family Controls (Distribution) request [7] for the app and each Screen Time extension [6]. Apple publishes no timeline; reports range from a few days to three weeks or more, Apple acknowledged a backlog in May 2026, and one bundle ID can stay pending after the others are approved [96][97] Community.
  3. Primary purpose ("Family Controls Framework" clause: 3.3.3(P) in the agreement of 29 October 2026 [101], 3.3.3(Q) in Apple's March 2026 notice [24]). The app's primary purpose must be parental supervision through Family Sharing, or focus, productivity and personal device-usage management; no ad blocking, organisations, or managing another adult's device. Usage data may serve only those controls (the parent in child mode, the user's own device in individual mode), never ads or data brokers [23].
    Risk for apps whose main purpose is something else (for example fitness): present the app as personal device-usage management in the request, the listing and the review notes, and make the feature prominent. Apple could still decline; the fallback is to ship without the feature while the request is pending.
  4. Guidelines [22]. 4.10: built-in capabilities, the Screen Time APIs named, can't be monetized. 3.1.1: paid unlocks use in-app purchase, and an app's own unlock mechanisms (QR codes named) can't unlock paid content; charge for the app's own features, never for "unlock blocking". 5.5: MDM is for enterprise, education and government, and in limited cases parental-control or device-security companies.

11.3 Privacy, law and honest wording

  • On-device only: rules, usage minutes, tokens and package names never leave the phone, and no analytics event contains an app identity. A one-screen explanation comes before each permission.
  • Adults only in the MVP. A child mode would bring the amended US COPPA rule (fully enforceable since 22 April 2026) [114] and the UK Children's Code, which requires an obvious sign to a monitored child; the ICO says this guidance is under review after the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 [116].
  • Texas SB 2420 applies to every app available in Texas, adult-only or not (in force since the Fifth Circuit stay of 5 June 2026): use the store's age category where needed, keep the age rating current, and ask for parental consent again when a minor's app changes significantly (an age-rating change counts) [115][139]. Tools: Apple's Declared Age Range and Significant Change APIs; Google's Play Age Signals API [140].
  • Coming: California's Digital Age Assurance Act from 1 January 2027 (request the OS or store age signal) [141]; Utah from 6 May 2027 and Louisiana from 1 July 2027 [115].
Honest wording: "This blocks the apps you choose and makes switching it off take effort. You can always remove Screen Time access in Settings or delete the app; if you do, it will show in your history." Never promise "can't be bypassed", "exact screen time" on iPhone, or "blocks every app" (50 app tokens per store on iOS, about a second of delay on Android).

13Recommendations and roadmap

Ship the MVP in section 4: real blocking (Level 3) with an opt-in Strict mode (Level 4). Skip Level 5. Order: before-launch steps (4.4), M1–M10, device pass and closed testing, launch; then M11–M16 (4.6), the shield earn menu and Android website blocking.

13.1 Long-term maintainability

  • Keep all rules in one shared engine; native code mirrors only "what's blocked now" through the plan. Gate features by OS version: 17.4 (thresholds), 26.4 (menu), 26.5 (open app, token expiry).
  • Track store build rules (Xcode 26 since 28 April 2026, Play target API 36 since 31 August 2026) [143][144], and rerun the device pass on every iOS and Android beta.
