5iOS in depth

An App Store app has exactly one supported way to restrict other apps: Apple's Screen Time API (iOS 15+ for parental control, iOS 16+ for an adult's own phone) [2][20][21]. It needs no device management, but distribution needs Apple's approval (11.1).

5.3 What ManagedSettings can restrict

An MVP needs only the shield group: applications, applicationCategories, webDomains and webDomainCategories [83]. Other groups exist (hiding apps [81], denying app removal [11], forcing automatic date and time [82]), but which work under individual authorization is undocumented; deny-removal is "not guaranteed" [26].

5.4 Shields and the picker

  • Block screen: title, subtitle, colours, an icon and two buttons [12]. The extension is the only place the app sees the blocked app's name.
  • Buttons: close, defer or no action [14]; up to 3 menu items on 26.4 [13]; open the app that applied the shield on 26.5 [14].
  • Websites: Safari and browsers that report through STWebpageController [83][84]. Brave, Firefox and sometimes Chrome reportedly aren't covered, while the webContent filter reportedly reaches them [126][127] Community. Shield other browser apps too.
  • Limits: category and .all shields skip apps in the user's Always Allowed list (Phone is always in it) [95], and the app itself is exempt from .all [117]; whether Settings can be shielded is Unverified. The picker reportedly returns fresh tokens on every "Done" [100] Community.

5.6 App limits, step by step

An iPhone app limit ("Instagram, 30 minutes a day") is two pieces: a usage event that wakes the monitor extension when the minutes are used, and a named store of its own that shields only that limit's apps. iOS does the counting; the app never reads the minutes directly [17][152].

StepWhat happens
1 · Set the limitThe app registers a DeviceActivityEvent for the picked apps, categories and sites, with a threshold of the minutes left today, inside a schedule that ends at midnight [16][17]. What counts is the time those items are on screen, added together [152]. With includesPastActivity: false only use after registering counts, so the app passes the minutes still left; true reaches back to the nearest whole hour [153].
2 · ProgressOptional extra events (for example every 15 minutes) wake the extension so the app can show "15 of 30 min". The steps are the app's choice, not an iOS limit.
3 · WarningA schedule warningTime of 5 minutes calls eventWillReachThresholdWarning at 25 of 30 minutes, for a "5 minutes left" notice [154][155]. It isn't sent when a threshold fires early [90] Community.
4 · Limit reachedeventDidReachThreshold wakes the extension [18]. It checks the event is believable, then puts the tokens in the limit's store. The shield covers the app at once, even while it's open; other apps stay usable. Up to 50 stores, the most restrictive wins [21].
5 · Block screen"Instagram is done for today", with the strictness level's way out (M8): "1 more minute" in Nudge, the emergency unlock in Standard, Close only in Strict. Earned time doesn't open a limited app.
6 · MidnightThe day schedule's start wakes the extension, which empties the store and registers a fresh event. An event fires once per schedule interval, so a never-ending schedule doesn't reset each day [156] Community.
TopicWhat to know
OpensNot possible: iOS reports minutes, never app launches [17]. "3 opens a day" is Android only (M11).
How manyLimits add events, not schedules, so the 20-schedule budget is untouched [16]. Each store takes up to 50 app tokens; one report says 50 across all stores [10][27]. Apple documents no cap on events per schedule.
AccuracyThe threshold is set in minutes, but iOS can be wrong in both directions. Early: on iOS 26.0–26.6 and 27, events can fire seconds after registering; it looked fixed in the 26.5 betas but returned in the public release, and was still reported in August 2026 [25] Community. Overcounting: since iOS 17.6.1, limits can fill faster than real use; limits on categories count Safari and the site being visited twice. Apple called it a bug with no workaround [90][91] Apple staff.
DefenceTreat a threshold as a wake-up, not proof: reject one that claims more minutes than have passed since registering, re-register the rest under a new generation name so stale events are ignored, and cap the retries [157] Community. This catches early events but not overcounting (the extension can't measure another app's use), so a limit can still close a few minutes early or late. Present limits as approximate.
ChangesLowering a limit applies at once: re-register with the new minutes left. Raising it is making things easier and follows the strictness rules (M8).
Ways aroundThe same as any iOS block: revoking access or deleting the app (5.2, section 8).

5.8 Recent changes, and what iOS can't do

2025 to November 2026: 26.4 added the shield submenu and EU usage data [5][13]; 26.5 added opening the app from the shield, token expiry and store management [14][15]; the March 2026 agreement added the primary-purpose rule (11.1) [23][24]. iOS 27 adds no developer APIs, only Apple's own parental "Time Allowances" [33], and App Review has no Screen Time-specific changes; 4.10 still applies [22].

Not possible: turning on Do Not Disturb or Focus (no API [86]; Focus filters only react when the user turns a Focus on [32][122], and the app can only read whether one is on [87]). MDM for consumers: it is for enterprise, education and government, and in limited cases parental-control or device-security companies with Apple's approval [22]; consumer supervision isn't practical [31].
